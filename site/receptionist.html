How a call actually goes

Someone rings your number. Here's the whole thing.

StepWhat happens
1Your line rings. It picks up with your greeting, in your voice — warm and brief, or formal and precise, however you set it.
2The caller says what they want. Before anything else, we check whether this is something an AI should be handling at all.
3If it's an appointment, it checks your real availability and offers times spread across different days — not three slots in the same afternoon.
4They pick one. It books, confirms out loud, and the appointment is in your calendar before they hang up.
5If it can't help — a price negotiation, a complaint, anything medical or legal — it transfers to you, or takes a message marked urgent.
6You get the outcome and a full transcript. Every call, whether it booked or not.

The four things it will never do

These aren't instructions we gave it. They're constraints in the code, which is a different and much stronger thing.

  • It will never double-book you Two people asking for the same 2pm in the same second is exactly what happens when you get busy. Our database refuses the second one outright — no race, no "we'll sort it out later."
  • It will never invent an appointment time It can only offer slots it was handed from your real calendar. If it tries to make one up, it books nothing instead. Nobody shows up to a locked door.
  • It will never handle an emergency Words like "chest pain" or "emergency" route to a human before the AI is asked what to do. It doesn't depend on the AI noticing.
  • It will never pretend to be a person Ask it directly and it says it's an AI assistant, plainly. Callers deserve a straight answer to a straight question.
And one more, because it's the failure we care most about

If it mishears you — a bad line, an accent, background noise — it asks you to repeat rather than guessing. Booking someone for the wrong Tuesday because a transcription was 40% confident is the kind of mistake nobody finds out about until the no-show.

Good fit / bad fit

This works well for

Appointment-driven businesses where a missed call is a lost booking: salons, tattoo studios, dental and medical practices, trades, clinics, repair shops, consultants. Anyone whose phone rings after they've stopped answering it.

This is a bad fit for

Anything where a wrong answer is dangerous — emergency services, crisis lines, medical triage. Businesses whose calls are mostly complex negotiation. And anyone who wants it to convincingly pass as a human, which it won't do and we won't build.
